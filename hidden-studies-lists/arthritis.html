Every study here was pulled straight from PubMed or ClinicalTrials.gov, so every link is real. The text under each study is the study's own conclusion, copied word for word from the official record. Yoga or Strengthening Exercise for Knee Osteoarthritis: A Randomized Clinical Trial.
PubMed · JAMA network open, 2025 · Randomized Controlled Trial

In this randomized clinical trial, yoga did not significantly reduce knee pain compared with strengthening exercises. However, yoga was found to be noninferior to strengthening exercises, suggesting that integrating yoga as an alternative or complementary exercise option in clinical practice may help in managing knee OA.

Rheumatoid arthritis and dietary interventions: systematic review of clinical trials.
PubMed · Nutrition reviews, 2021 · Systematic Review

Vitamin D and marine omega 3 fatty acid supplementation and incident autoimmune disease: VITAL randomized controlled trial.
PubMed · BMJ (Clinical research ed.), 2022 · Randomized Controlled Trial

Vitamin D supplementation for five years, with or without omega 3 fatty acids, reduced autoimmune disease by 22%, while omega 3 fatty acid supplementation with or without vitamin D reduced the autoimmune disease rate by 15% (not statistically significant). Both treatment arms showed larger effects than the reference arm (vitamin D placebo and omega 3 fatty acid placebo).

Dietary factors and risk of gout and hyperuricemia: a meta-analysis and systematic review.
PubMed · Asia Pacific journal of clinical nutrition, 2018 · Meta-Analysis, Systematic Review

The risk of hyperuricemia and gout is positively correlated with the intake of red meat, seafoods, alcohol or fructose, and negatively with dairy products or soy foods. High-purine vegetables showed no association with hyperuricemia, but negative association with gout. Coffee intake is negatively associated with gout risk, whereas it may be associated with increased hyperuricemia risk in women but decreased risk in men.

From this systematic review, we conclude that acupuncture may be effective at relieving CKP 12 weeks after acupuncture administration, based on the current evidence and our protocol. Yoga in autoimmune disorders: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials.
PubMed · Annals of behavioral medicine : a publication of the Society of Behavioral Medicine, 2025 · Systematic Review

This systematic review found a positive impact of Yoga on various physical, biochemical, and psychological measures in 5 common ADs. Yoga may be considered an adjunct therapy in the conventional management of ADs. An autoimmune disorder is a medical condition in which the immune system mistakenly attacks the body’s tissues. As a mind-body intervention, yoga has shown beneficial effects on immune function. In this review, we examined 26 randomized controlled trials to evaluate the benefits of yoga for individuals with autoimmune disorders. Five most common autoimmune disorders—multiple sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, inflammatory bowel disease, and periodontitis -published through mid-2024 were included. This article concludes that practicing yoga (at least twice weekly for 8 weeks or longer) can improve balance, reduce fatigue and depression, and enhance quality of life in patients with multiple sclerosis. In rheumatoid arthritis, yoga appears to lower inflammation and reduce depressive symptoms. Furthermore, yoga has demonstrated positive effects on disease activity, physical functioning, and quality of life in patients with ankylosing spondylitis. It has also been found to be beneficial for patients with inflammatory bowel disease and periodontitis.
